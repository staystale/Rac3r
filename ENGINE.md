# ENGINE - architecture overview

How the game is put together. Details live in the code comments of the file named; this page is the map.
Older design notes and measurements are in the git history of this file.

## Run, build, check

- `node serve.mjs` serves the game on http://localhost:8080. It transforms TypeScript with esbuild per
  request, so there is no build step while developing.
- `npm run build` (`tools/build.mjs`) makes the one bundled file for itch.io.
- `npm run verify` (`tools/verify.mjs`): syntax, encoding, tsc and lint (`tools/lint.mjs`, the CLAUDE.md
  section 2 rules).
- `npm run suite` runs the quick checks with pass / fail; `node tools/runall.mjs --full` adds the slow ones.
- Proof that a refactor changed nothing (save the reference on the base commit, then compare):
  - `tools/worldhash.mjs` - every level's data after load (`--highway 20` drives 20 km of motorway).
  - `tools/simhash.mjs` - every car's state every 0.5 s over 13 deterministic runs (AI, traffic, crash, cones).
  - `tools/renderhash.mjs` - the canvas pixels at fixed moments of 14 deterministic runs (CPU raster).
  - All three use `tools/lib/determinism.mjs`: a seeded `Math.random`, a fake clock and a stepped
    `requestAnimationFrame`.
- `tools/zoomcheck.mjs` measures how much the same patch of ground changes between zooms.

## One frame

`game/loop.ts`, driven by `requestAnimationFrame`:

1. Input: `input.ts` (or the autopilot / demo driver) gives one input state.
2. Fixed-step simulation: an accumulator runs `game/sim.ts` in steps of `PHYSICS_STEP_SECONDS` (1/120 s):
   the player car (`vehicle.ts step`), AI cars (`game/ai.ts`), highway streaming and traffic, contact,
   damage, rollovers, loose objects, wear, race rules and timing.
3. Interpolation of every car between the last two steps.
4. Draw: `renderer.frame()` (world into the art-pixel buffer, then to the screen), then the HUD.
5. Audio update and the fps meter.

## The car (`vehicle.ts` and `physics/`)

A top-down two-axle model with four tyre temperatures. `step(car, input, dt)`, in order:

1. `steerFrontWheels` - steering with speed-sensitive lock and self-aligning (`physics/powertrain.ts`).
2. `runPowertrain` - reverse, gears and shifting, engine rpm, throttle and brake demand.
3. `computeAxleLoads`, `computeLongitudinalDemand` - weight transfer and drive / brake per axle
   (`physics/loads.ts`).
4. `computeWheelForces` - each axle's WHEEL SPEED is state (`car.wU`, m/s at the tread), turned by the
   engine (its inertia through the gearing while the clutch is in), the brakes and the tyre, in 4
   implicit substeps (`physics/wheelspin.ts`). One combined law: longitudinal slip and slip angle,
   each over its peak (later on loose ground: `peakK`), make one slip whose force follows the lateral
   curve's shape (`curveFactor`: up to the peak, down to `slideGrip`), split by each one's share - pure
   cornering is the old curve, a locked wheel slides straight on, a wheel held at the peak keeps some
   steering. ABS dumps / snaps back / creeps (9-18 pulses a s); the rear watches its whole slip (EBD).
   AWD axles are coupled (a viscous centre). Revs and the rev limiter follow the driven wheels. Grip
   by load / aero / temperature, relaxation and ploughing: `physics/tyres.ts`. (Replaced 11-02: a
   friction circle + a sliding law with the slip GUESSED from demand vs grip, and an ABS that capped
   the brake under grip - locked = ABS stops, no steering under ABS; `tools/scratch/_rootbench.mjs`.)
5. `ploughingForces`, `stabilityControl` - understeer plough and the ESC / TCS aids.
6. `bodyAccelerations`, `integrateYaw`, `lowSpeedBlend` - body motion; a blend near standstill.
7. `updateTyreTemps` (`physics/tyreheat.ts`), `advanceCar`, `writeTelemetry`.

The mods seam: every car has `car.mods` (`cars.ts defaultMods`: power, shiftTime, inertia, gripLat,
gripLong, brakeTorque, mass, rigidity), all 1.0 by default. Upgrades and tyres change these multipliers;
the model reads them and nothing else.

Surfaces come from the ground under each wheel (`world/wear.ts groundAt`): the surface's grip and drag,
any cover (snow, ice, puddle) and the wear state of the cell. Grip is one sample per axle (`car.surface`
is the rear's); each wheel's MARK and SPRAY come from the ground under that wheel, read by `stepWear`
before its pass (`car.wheelSurf`, carWheels order; `world/wheels.ts CORNER_OF_WHEEL` maps a wheel
to its damage corner). A lost wheel's tyre reads 0 C and its axle's temperature grip is the other
tyre's.

Bushes are ground too: the `bush` surface (its `brush` drag grows with speed,
`physics/tyres.ts`), laid as patches by `world/bushplan.ts` from each level's plan
(`world/levels/bushes.ts`), flattened to long grass by passes (`wear.ts BASE_WEAR`); a wheel
entering a bush cell may puncture or scratch that corner (`world/bushes.ts`). Drawn by
`render/ground/bushes.ts` (squares in the trees' pixel style: shadow, lit squares, shaded side)
over the `bush` art. A few leaf tufts per bush are drawn over the cars
(`render/bushtufts.ts`, in the overhead pass).

Car systems (C): every puncture source (big hits in `damage.ts`,
heat / wear bursts, debris, nails, bushes) ends in `damage.ts`, which starts a LEAK in
`physics/tyrepressure.ts` - a burst flat in ~0.5 s with a sharp pull, a slow one (`car.burstSlow`)
over 10-30 s. Each tyre's pressure sets its axle's grip (1 -> 0.7 flat -> 0.45 on the rim), a pull
toward it and extra rolling drag, folded into `car.wreck` by `damage.ts derive`; driven flat
the rubber comes off by a chance rising with the metres run flat (half by ~1 km). `damage.ts stepDamage` runs it each physics step
(player `game/sim.ts`, AI `game/ai.ts`). METAL ON HARD GROUND (`world/metalscrape.ts`): a rim, or
a rolled body sliding (`roll.ts car.sparks`), sparks and may break each new wear cell a grade
(race tarmac -> tarmac -> broken) through `gougeCell`; a rollover impact has its own chance; a
rim scores a pale mark (`MARK_GOUGE`, `marks.ts`). Drawn by `render/tyrelook.ts` (the tyre
squashes progressively with its air, a rim bare), puffs / shreds / rim sparks in
`render/particles.ts`, the HUD tyre ring by pressure (`hud/gauges.ts`), `?dev=1` line `air`.
Tyre spikes (a police stinger, `world/stingers.ts`, drawn by `render/stingers.ts`) on the Lab and Test Track burst every wheel that rolls on. The Lab's
flip ramp (`world/flipramps.ts`, an armco end; `roll.ts tripCheck` asks it) rolls a car run up it.
ENGINE: `physics/engineheat.ts` keeps each car's engine C, radiator
water, oil and a seize timer, stepped by `damage.ts stepDamage` (every car from the start). Heat
in by throttle x revs (`car.throttleUsed`, set by `vehicle.ts step`); out through the radiator
(thermostat from 87 C, the water left, airflow by speed + rain + wheel splashes) and the block.
A front hit (`damage.ts hit`: the harder-hit front corner's share 0.15+) cracks the radiator
(empty in 4 min .. 40 s), a hard one (0.35+) or a rollover impact may crack the sump. The
leak is WATER: ~60 wear cells a radiator under the nose become the tyres' water (`world/puddles.ts`
rules, the GPU field's look), closer together the worse the leak. Power fades 115 -> 130 C to half (folded into
`car.wreck.power` by `derive`); 10 s at 130 C or with no oil SEIZES it (power 0; an AI resets:
`tooInjured`). Speedo lights `hud/enginelights.ts`; exhaust, backfires, steam and engine smoke
`render/enginesmoke.ts` (pipe spots per car kind, `EXHAUST_SPOTS`); `?dev=1` line `eng`.
OIL: `world/oil.ts` - a wear cell's cover 'oil' like the tyres' water
(`wet.ts oilSurface`: a third of the base's grip; drawn by the GPU field as kind 5, like ice
but purple). Spilled by a cracked sump (`engineheat.ts`), a big hit or a rollover impact
(`damage.ts` -> `crashOil` / `rollOil`); soaked up by loose ground; never dries. A pass
through it may spread it a cell the way the wheel went (`wear.ts stepWear` notes each wheel's
motion); oil beside water runs into it 50:50 a second (`stepOil`); each spread a generation
weaker (`cell.oilGen`).
DEBRIS: `world/debris.ts`. A corner's hit of 0.3+ may knock off a
part (`damage.ts hardHitCorners -> crashDebris`): a bumper (end-on, one an end), a wing or a door
panel, queued on the car (`partSpawn`) and thrown by `game/sim.ts throwParts` into the loose
objects (`cones.ts`; DEBRIS kinds `bumper`, `wing`, `panel` in the car's paint and trim,
`render/objects.ts`); past 24 the oldest fade out (`stepParts`, `cones.cull`). A hit of 0.15+
may break a window (not the track car, banger, grass car): wear cells take the cover 'glass'
(no grip change; GPU field kind 6, specks whose glints follow the camera); the first tyre pass
sweeps it and may puncture that tyre (`world/pass.ts PASS`: the wheel making the pass).

Highway hedges: `highway/hedges.ts` lays one continuous line of square
bushes (the same `bush` patches as the other levels) round each farm field's long-grass border, streamed with
the field (laid in `highway/fields.ts`, dropped by the slide's prune and `clearFields`) and
indexed through `world/load.ts indexNewBushes` (added a field at a time) and `reindexBushes`.

Drawing uses its own random numbers (`render/shared.ts fxRandom`, a hashed count), never
`Math.random`, so no look can shift the physics' random stream. Sparks (`render/particles.ts`) glow in
the night light map (`render/light.ts drawSparkGlows`).

## Drawing (`render.ts` and `render/`)

- Pixel art: the world is drawn into a low-resolution art-pixel buffer (about 15 art px per metre at
  zoom 1) and blitted to the screen with smoothing off. The camera is snapped to whole art pixels and the
  leftover fraction is applied when blitting. The HUD is drawn afterwards at full resolution.
- `render/camera.ts`: follow with look-ahead, speed zoom (`ZOOM_MIN` 0.30), the level fly-in and the
  whole-level overview picture used during it.
- `render/tiles.ts`: what lies over the floor and zones (track ribbon, lanes, patches, covers,
  wear, kerbs) is painted into cached tiles at ONE density (the zoom-1 step) within a time budget
  per frame (`tilesBudget.ts`); the GPU ground scales them to any zoom and draws each at its exact
  place. Tiles under new wear go stale and are repainted within the budget (`tilesWear.ts`).
  `tilesPalette.ts` builds the palette the GPU snaps to; `tilesShade.ts` the hill shading's
  heights and bands (drawn by `glshade.ts`).
- `render/ground/` paints into tiles: `art.ts` the per-surface art table, `prims.ts` pattern primitives,
  `zones.ts`, `covers.ts` (puddles, snow), `wear.ts` (worn cells, bumps, runoff).
- `render/textures.ts`: each surface's pattern is painted once at `TEX_PPM` (the car sprites' pixel
  size), seamless, and filled scaled to the zoom.
- Layers drawn each frame over the tiles: `road.ts` (lanes, kerbs, paint, slip roads), `marks.ts`,
  `objects.ts` (cones, tyres, tape, debris), `props.ts` + `props/` (fence, barriers, buildings, scenery,
  bridges), `cars.ts` with `carsprite.ts` (one cached sprite per livery) and `cardamage.ts`,
  `particles.ts`.
- Barriers (`render/props/fence.ts drawPropsBarriers`): one off-screen by its whole box (cached,
  `_rbb`) is skipped. Each is also cut into stretches of ~30 m with their
  own boxes (`_stretches`, cut again when a dent or tear gives it new points); only the stretches
  on screen are stroked (each run of them as one line, so the joins are the whole line's) and
  posted (each from the running distance the whole line has there: the same posts). Drawn whole:
  the pit wall (its painted blocks are one dash pattern along the line) and the rock wall.
- `render/light.ts`: the night layer, the ONLY light system (cars and props paint lamps, never
  light) - a light map the art buffer's size, the dark filled, headlight beams, lamp pools and the
  cars' lamp glows rubbed out, then the pools' warm tint and the glows' colour laid in; laid over
  the screen canvas once a frame under the HUD (nothing by day). The light level is
  `world/daylight.ts` (`?night=` fixes it, a test tool); the AI and traffic run headlights by it
  (`game/sim.ts updateFieldHeadlights`; the player's LIGHTS are their own).
- Lamps: `world/lamps.ts` makes the level's street lights (`lamps` data) and floodlight pylons
  into lamps with a solid post (`barriers.ts solidClamp`), lit from dusk; the night layer draws
  their pools, `render/props/lamps.ts` the street lights (overhead layer). A street course
  (`Level.streetLamps`, L.A. Lively) gets the plan's street lights along every straight of the
  lap, not only the start straight (`world/lampplan.ts`). The highway lays its
  own as the road grows (`highway/roadlamps.ts`: one every 34 m on alternating sides, kept
  in `level.lamps` by road distance and dropped behind with the window);
  `lamps.ts` keeps each streamed entry the same lamp, damage and all, while it is loaded.
- Solid lookups: `world/solidgrid.ts` files the level's solids and lamp posts by 16 m cell
  (`core/cellgrid.ts`, the one grid helper: the cover index and the bump grid use it too), so a
  car's clamp and a moving loose object test the few near them, in the lists' order (identical
  answers). Rebuilt when the level, its solids or its lamps change (the highway sets
  `solidsDirty` at each window rebuild). Highway: 4.3 -> 0.3 us a car-step with ~250 solids.
- Lane lookups: `world/zones.ts laneAt` files each lane's segments by 8 m cell over its box (built
  on first use, again when the highway rebuilds a lane's segments), so a lookup tests only its
  cell's segments, in order (identical answers: `tools/scratch/_cclaneat.mjs`).
- The game clock: `world/clock.ts` - a global time of day / season / moon
  (1 game day = 1 real hour, a season 7 days), saved in its own storage key, run by the sim step
  in events (`game/loop.ts`); it sets the sky (`daylight.ts setSky`: darkness and the dark's
  colour) that the night layer, the lamps and the headlights follow. TEST MODE setup: TIME OF DAY.
- `render/glground.ts`: THE ground (the game needs WebGL2; the iPhone's Safari has it since iOS
  15): the cached tiles are drawn by WebGL2 on a canvas UNDER the screen canvas, at full screen
  resolution with a sharp-pixel filter (crisp art pixels, no pixels dropped when the zoom changes);
  the art buffer is cleared to transparent so the GPU ground shows through, and everything else stays
  2D. A lost context (Safari under memory pressure, the app in the background) is rebuilt when the
  browser gives it back (`watchContext`); meanwhile the ground keeps its last frame.
- `render/glpaint.ts`: the floor and the zones painted on that GPU canvas every frame in world space
  at TEX_PPM texels per metre (the surface textures sampled per pixel, the tile palette applied by a
  lookup texture); `ground/zones.ts listGroundPatches` lists them, and the tiles then leave those
  fills transparent (`view.fillsOnGpu`). `glshade.ts` the hill shading, `glfield.ts` the cover
  field over it.
- Sharp cars (render.ts `drawSharpCarLayers`): the cars on the full-resolution
  screen canvas, not the art buffer. The art buffer under the cars is blitted, each car is painted
  facing +x into its own picture (`cars.ts paintCarPicture`, the same `drawPixelCar`, each sprite px
  a whole-number block) and turned and scaled onto the screen with smoothing; the art buffer then
  takes the overhead props and is blitted again. `render/glprogram.ts` builds the ground's shader.
- `render/screen.ts`: the screen canvas passes of a frame (the art buffer blits, the sharp cars, and
  the skid marks drawn on the screen between the deck and the layers above).

## Levels

Hand-made circuits in `world/levels/` (data: centreline, zones, props, barriers; `kit.ts` decorates a
traced track), generated stages from `stagegen.ts`, and the endless highway (`highway/`). Every name is a
parody pun (CLAUDE.md). A level is loaded by `world/load.ts`; `world/state.ts` holds the active one and its
lists (zones, bumps, props, barriers), which the physics and the renderer both read, so what you see is
what you drive on.

A circuit is its real venue (`world/venue.ts` and `venue*.ts`, from OpenStreetMap; `levels/brands-venue.ts`
is the spec of one, `levels/castle-cum-venue.ts` another): `venuescenery.ts` the
scenery, `venueedge.ts` the barriers (traps, real tyre walls, a venue's real rails where it follows them,
catch fences), `venueroads.ts` / `venuejoin.ts` the roads, `venuepit.ts` the pit lane, `tools/venueaudit.mjs`
and `tools/venueplan.mjs` the checks. Its road bridges and tunnels are LEVELS a car can drive:
`world/crossings.ts` `clampWorld` (every car's step, before `applyGround`) puts a car on a crossing when
its centre comes in over an end between the walls and off when it passes an end; on one it meets only the
crossing's walls, drives on its deck (`crossinggeo.ts`: tarmac, straight from end height to end height, no
wear), touches only cars on it (`contact.ts`), stamps no marks, throws no effects, counts no lap
(`game/order.ts`), has its own ground (`deckwear.ts`: the wear cells filed far off the map - its water, oil and
glass look, grip and dry as on any ground), and is drawn over the bridge or, in a tunnel, only outside its underground stretch
(`render/screen.ts`); its tyre marks are its own layer, drawn the same way (`marks.ts`). `tools/crossingcheck.mjs` drives every one.
A lap that crosses itself (Snoozuka, the ROC stadiums: `world/venuecross.ts`) runs under in a cut whose portals are turned along
the road at each end and whose walls follow the road's own path (the bent-tunnel crossing, as Moanaco's tunnel); the bridge's
parapets stop at the portals' walls. The open cut is the portal's ramp (`render/props/venueprops.ts drawTunnel`, 8 m of
its 9 m): its floor in five shades darkening to the mouth, painted over the ground beside the road; the race road's
part of it is darkened by black at rising shares instead, so the road's own surface, wear and lines show through
(`drawRampOverRoad`; the floor beside the road keeps the opaque shades). A ROC stadium (`tools/scratch/_rocdata.mjs`, one generator for all) crosses on a straight
X (its portals square with their lane; its barrier ends carried on to the portals' wall corners, `world/venuetidy.ts`) and races on a SPLIT
START (`Level.splitStart`): its cars on the path's two halves, each timed over its laps from its own line (`game/grid.ts`,
`game/order.ts` progressOf). A stadium may dress its own bowl (`VenueSpec.bowlLook`: the stands' seat colours, the roof ring's
steel lattice - `world/venuebowl.ts`, drawn by `drawGrandstand` / `drawBlock`), lay litter by hand (`VenueSpec.litter`: loose
debris kinds, tidied as every loose object) and floodlight pylons (landmarks of kind `pylon`, lamps as any level's).
A stadium's tidied edge is made even (`VenueSpec.evenEdge`, `world/stadiumtidy.ts`: Armco and catch
fences eased by Taubin smoothing, free ends carried on to the run beside them, each end at a tunnel mouth laid
along the road out of it, `world/portalflare.ts`) and it gets a dressing pass (`VenueSpec.dress`:
props and loose objects from the kits in `tools/scratch/_dresskits.mjs`, laid after the boards and kept clear of them and the barriers).
A run the tidy carried round a portal's wall to its lintel corner, with no run on that wall's mouth corner, is laid to the
mouth instead (`portalflare.ts mouthFor`: L.A. Lively's south portal). L.A. Lively gets the same
pass: `evenEdge` and its own dressing from `_lalivelydata.mjs` (a paddock in the lot east of the convention hall, a TV
platform and fans by the jump, fans by the start, show cars in the infield). No level fixes its
own hour: every event runs on the world clock, or TEST MODE's START HOUR (`game/menuflow.ts`).
A venue's RALLYCROSS layout (`world/levels/rallycross-venue.ts` rallycrossLevel: the joker rule, the bales, the rallycross bushes
unless the venue is `bushless`) takes its joker from another layout's loop (`VenueSpec.joker`, `world/venuejoker.ts`), the lane on
that loop's own surface at its middle (a dirt joker: L.A. Lively's). No bale or tyre stands on the
joker lane's road (clearJokerLane: the corner bales went round the lap's outside where the joker branches off). L.A. Lively (`tools/scratch/_lalivelydata.mjs`)
is the first: a street figure of eight crossing at 90 deg, its joker's gap jump the terrain's ramp and table pieces laid five side
by side across the road (cos^2 pieces half their width apart sum to a flat full height).
Under `?xgaymsloose=1` it races a second layout from the same generator (`_lalivelydata.mjs loose` writes
`venues/lalivelyloose.ts`, the same export names; `lalively-venue.ts` picks one): the figure of eight, the crossing, the lots,
the joker and its jump kept, the corners mixed (a fast sweeper, a 15 m hairpin, a corner that tightens 28 -> 14 m, a kink),
the streets bending, the lines free across the lots; its lap length as laid (`LALIVELY_LAP_M`) calibrates it, its least
radius 10 m (`VenueSpec.minR`: the venues' 15.5 m opened the hairpin across its own legs).
