# Feature Spec: Dynamic Tire System, Fitment Constraints & Tuning Menu Integration

## 1. Core Overview
Introduce a modular tire simulation system where vehicles have restricted fitment pools based on class/chassis constraints. Tires will feature distinct thermal profiles, surface-dependent grip and degradation curves, and manufacturer variants. For testing, an in-menu selector will allow rapid cycling through valid tire setups for any active vehicle.

## 2. Fitment Rules & Compatibility Constraints
Tire selection is gated by vehicle chassis/class logic to prevent illogical setups while enabling valid conversions.

* **Compatibility Matrix:**
  * **Hatchback / Standard Road Cars:** Standard Road, Performance Street, Rally / Gravel, Mild Track Spec. *(Disallowed: Dedicated Formula/Open-wheel slicks, extreme prototype race tires).*
  * **Off-Road / Utility:** All-Terrain, Deep Sand/Mud, Heavy-Duty Off-Road, Standard Road.
  * **Track / Race Cars:** High-Performance Street, Semi-Slicks, Full Track Slicks, Wets.
* **Fitment Logic:** Each vehicle data definition should hold an array of compatible tire classes/tags (e.g., `compatible_tire_categories: ["road", "street_perf", "rally"]`).

## 3. Tire Physics: Thermal & Surface Dynamics
Each tire category adheres to distinct thermal operating windows, degradation rates, and surface interactions:

| Tire Category | Cold Behavior | Ideal Temp Range | Overheat Threshold & Penalty | Surface Sensitivity |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Standard Road** | Tolerant; grips decently right away. | Narrow advantage band; nominal grip boost when warm. | Overheats quickly under heavy slip/braking; noticeable degradation once past limit. | Optimized for tarmac/concrete; terrible on mud/sand. |
| **Performance / Track** | Very poor grip when cold; requires deliberate warmup. | Wide, high-grip operating window. | High thermal ceiling; resists degradation longer at elevated temps. | Requires smooth asphalt/concrete; ruined rapidly off-road. |
| **Rally / Gravel** | Consistent across broad temp range; slight grip deficit on clean tarmac. | Medium window, robust baseline. | Moderate resistance; handles variable heat without sudden drops. | Universal versatility; highly effective across dirt, loose gravel, and transition zones. |
| **Off-Road (Dirt/Sand)** | High baseline traction on soft/loose surfaces. | Optimized for churn/soft earth displacement. | Overheats rapidly and wears aggressively when driven on hard surfaces (tarmac, concrete, lab floor). | Extreme grip on loose terrain; squirmy and prone to thermal runaway on solid pavement. |

## 4. Brand & Manufacturer Architecture (Data Structure)
* Target count: ~10 distinct fictional or licensed manufacturers.
* Each brand provides variations in quality tier, compound focus, and specific specialties (e.g., Brand A specializes in wet/road longevity, Brand B specializes in sticky but fragile track compounds).
* Keep the data structure flexible so additional manufacturers and compound stats can be added as data assets without core refactoring.

## 5. UI / Tune Menu: Testing Implementation
* **Tune Menu Addition:** Add a **Tire Selection** entry to the vehicle tuning/garage interface.
* **Cycling Selector:** Provide a shuffle/cycle control (Previous / Next) displaying the currently mounted tire's:
  * Brand & Model Name
  * Category / Compound Type
  * Live mini-stats preview (Optimal Temp Range, Grip Index, Surface Rating)
* Filter the selector dynamically so it only displays compounds defined as compatible with the currently active car.

## 6. Initial Implementation Milestone (MVP)
To keep the immediate implementation light and focused for testing:
1. Implement the underlying data schema for tires (compound, thermal curve, compatibility tags).
2. Wire up the **Tune Menu selector** with real-time switching on the current test vehicles.
3. Build out just **two base reference profiles** for testing:
   * **Base Standard Road Tire**
   * **Base Rally Tire**
4. Verify thermal behavior differences, surface transitions (tarmac vs. loose surface), and fitment filtering before rolling out the full roster of 10 brands.

# Feature Spec: Track Builder Integration & Circuit Roster

## 1. System Overview & Level Building Pipeline
The track creation pipeline follows a strict two-pass construction model that integrates with the existing editor toolset:

### Pass 1: The Core Sub-Track (Invisible Spine)
* **Function**: An invisible spline loop that defines the foundational raceway path.
* **Responsibilities**:
  * **Timing & Triggers**: Generates lap triggers, sector splits, and checkpoints.
  * **AI Racing Line**: Serves as the navigation reference path for AI bots.
  * **Reset Logic**: Used by the vehicle recovery system to calculate track orientation and nearest valid respawn nodes when a car leaves the bounds or rolls over.

### Pass 2: Visible Surface & Feature Layer (Drawn on Top)
* **Function**: Applied directly over the core sub-track spline.
* **Customization Options**:
  * **Variable Width**: Taper track width per section.
  * **Surface Painting**: Paint modular surface segments (Tarmac, Dirt, Gravel, Concrete) to trigger the corresponding tire grip and thermal behaviors.
  * **Track Furniture**: Toggle rumble strips, kerbs, painted lines, runoff areas, and tire barriers.

## 2. Editor Tool Enhancements
To support this workflow, the in-game editor needs simple hooks:
1. **Spline Tool Mode**: Place, move, and snap core spline nodes to form the closed timing/reset loop.
2. **Surface Painter Mode**: Select a segment along the core line and apply surface types (e.g., Tarmac vs. Loose Gravel).
3. **Kerb / Furniture Brush**: Paint left/right kerbs, runoffs, or barriers directly along the road edges.
4. **Validation Test**: A one-click check that verifies the loop is closed, lap triggers are sequential, and AI reset normals point down the track.

## 3. Circuit Roster & Build Guides
These tracks are intended to be laid down using the invisible core spline first, then finished with the surface painter.

### Track 1: "Castle Cum" (Parody of Castle Combe)
* **Style**: Fast, flowing, old-school UK tarmac perimeter track.
* **Surface Breakdown**: 100% Tarmac. Heavy curb usage.
#### Build Guide:
1. **Core Sub-Track Path**:
   * Draw a high-speed clockwise loop with long straights and fast sweeps.
   * Add a fast kink off the pit straight leading to an uphill blind rise (Avon Crest).
   * Drop into a hard-braking 90° right (Quarry), followed by a sweeping back straight.
   * Add a sharp left-right chicane (The Esses), a wide left sweep (Hammerdown), a sharp 90° right (Tower), and another tight chicane (Bobbies) before the final long right sweeper (Camp).
2. **Surface Layering**:
   * Set entire base surface to **Tarmac**.
   * Paint prominent **Rumble Kerbs** on the inside and outside of *The Esses* and *Bobbies Chicane*.
   * Add grass/gravel runoff zones tightly hugging the outside of *Quarry* and *Camp*.

### Track 2: "Thruston" (Parody of Thruxton)
* **Style**: Ultra-fast, flowing airfield layout with relentless lateral Gs.
* **Surface Breakdown**: 100% Tarmac. High tire-heat generator.
#### Build Guide:
1. **Core Sub-Track Path**:
   * Draw a massive, sweeping clockwise oval-like loop.
   * After the main straight, create a technical right-left-right bowl section (The Complex).
   * Follow with huge, sustained, high-radius continuous right-hand turns (Noble, Goodwood, and Church) where cars stay pinned at top speed.
   * End the loop with a slow, tight stop-and-go chicane (Club) right before the finish straight.
2. **Surface Layering**:
   * Set entire base surface to **Tarmac**.
   * Keep track width wide (12m+) through the long sweeps to allow multiple slipstream lines.
   * Place heavy kerbing and tire markers strictly at the final *Club Chicane*.

### Track 3: "Slidden Hill" (Parody of Lydden Hill)
* **Style**: Classic mixed-surface Rallycross (RX) circuit with elevation changes and a Joker Lap.
* **Surface Breakdown**: ~60% Tarmac, ~40% Loose Gravel / Packed Dirt.
#### Build Guide:
1. **Core Sub-Track Path**:
   * Short, punchy clockwise circuit.
   * Downhill drop off the start line into a wide bowl hairpin (Canterbury Hairpin).
   * Steep uphill climb (Hairy Hill) cresting into a wide drift bend (Chessons Drift) returning to the pit straight.
   * **Joker Split**: Add a branched spline loop around the outside of *Chessons Drift* that adds 2–3 seconds to the lap time.
2. **Surface Layering**:
   * **Main Straight & Hill Climb**: Paint as **Tarmac**.
   * **Canterbury Hairpin**: Paint base as **Loose Gravel** with banked edges.
   * **Chessons Drift**: Paint as **Hard-Packed Dirt** to encourage high-angle slides.
   * **Joker Section**: Paint as deep, loose **Gravel** to heavily penalize pace while serving the penalty loop.

## 4. Implementation Priorities for Downstream Models
1. Ensure the core sub-track generator outputs clean checkpoint ordering (`start_finish` -> `sector_1` -> `sector_2`).
2. Verify that AI reset vectors use the core track's forward tangent so respawned cars face forward on the track.

# Gemini's notes on the user's ideas — Architectural Blindspots & Recommendations for the Roadmap

Reviewing the proposed systems (dynamic tires, sub-track level building, and the UI/garage state machine), there are several missing links that need addressing early to prevent major rewrites:

## 1. Real-Time Telemetry & Visual Feedback (Tire System)
* **Thermal HUD / Tire Widget:** If tire heat and surface degradation are simulated, the player needs a way to read that state during gameplay (e.g., a simple 4-wheel UI icon showing tire temp via color shifts: cold blue -> optimal green -> overheating red, alongside a surface indicator).
* **Audio & Particle FX Hooks:**
  * Tarmac slip needs distinct tire squeal audio and smoke particles.
  * Loose gravel and dirt require kick-up particulate emitters and abrasive spray sound effects.
  * Off-road tires on tarmac need a specific high-frequency scrub sound and rapid smoke generation when sliding.

## 2. Track Data & AI Boundary Definition
* **Track Boundaries & Off-Track Detection:** The sub-track spline defines the line and resets, but the system needs explicit off-track boundaries (distance threshold from spline center or secondary boundary splines) to trigger automatic resets, lap invalidations, or slowdown penalties.
* **Surface Tag Colliders:** Ensure the surface painter applies physics material tags (friction/drag multipliers) directly to the road mesh colliders so the car's physics sweep can detect what compound is under each wheel individually.

## 3. Game State & In-Session Overlay Handling
* **Test Mode Hot-Swapping State Machine:** Swapping cars or tires mid-session without reloading the scene requires a robust reset function:
  * Cache current vehicle transform/respawn point.
  * Destroy or despawn old vehicle controller and physics body.
  * Instantiate new vehicle prefab/data, inherit velocity = 0, and cleanly rebind player input, camera target, and HUD telemetry listeners.
* **Persistence & Save Schema:** Define where `player_profile` data is stored (Local Storage, JSON file, PlayerPrefs) so garage changes and unlocks persist across play sessions.

# AI Driver Architecture: Track Awareness, Dynamic Handling & Error Simulation

## 1. Track & Environmental Awareness
The AI must not drive purely on static, scripted rails. It needs situational awareness matching human perception:

* **Dual-Layer Track Perception:**
  * **Core Sub-Track (Macro Line):** Uses the invisible spline for global orientation, calculating apex approaches, target corner speeds, and sector pacing.
  * **Visual Surface Layer (Micro Adaptation):** Senses the actual physical surface under its wheels (Tarmac, Gravel, Dirt).
* **Surface Predictive Handling:**
  * AI adjusts target braking points and turn-in speeds ahead of surface transitions (e.g., braking earlier on gravel than on tarmac).
  * If fitted with mismatched tires (e.g., road slicks on wet dirt), the AI adjusts grip expectations accordingly rather than magically having baseline traction.
* **Track Boundaries & Corridors:** AI tracks its lateral distance from track edges and kerbs, attempting to stay within limits during normal racing while exploiting kerbs dynamically.

## 2. Human Error & Mistake Simulation System
To avoid "robotic slot-car" behavior, the AI requires a dynamic error-injection model:

* **Stress & Pressure Metric:**
  * Proximity of opponents (tailgating, side-by-side moves) increases an internal `pressure` variable.
  * Elevated pressure increases the probability of human-like driving errors.
* **Error Types:**
  * **Late Braking / Lockups:** Overcooking the entry into heavy braking zones and running wide into runoff areas.
  * **Snap Oversteer / Slides:** Applying too much throttle on low-grip surfaces or unsettling the car over aggressive kerbs, forcing recovery counter-steering.
  * **Compromised Exits:** Missing the optimal apex when defending or battling side-by-side, resulting in slower exit speeds down the straight.
  * **Unforced Driver Errors:** Low-probability random mistakes (spinning out, clipping a barrier, or carrying too much speed over blind crests).

## 3. Physical Presence, Collision & Damage Integration
AI vehicles must operate under the exact same physics and collision rules as the player:

* **Full Rigid-Body Physics:**
  * AI cars are fully simulated physical colliders, not kinematic or immune objects.
  * Equal collision reactions: A player hitting an AI car pushes and deflects it; an AI hitting the player transfers impulse proportionally based on mass, speed, and contact angle.
* **Spatial Avoidance & Racecraft:**
  * Raycasts / proximity colliders detect nearby cars to avoid gratuitous ramming while allowing aggressive, wheel-to-wheel door rubbing.
  * Awareness of blind spots during defensive maneuvers and overtakes.
* **Damage System Compatibility:**
  * Mechanical and visual damage states apply equally to AI.
  * Collisions deform pixel bodywork/panels and trigger mechanical penalties (steering pull, suspension damage, engine power loss) that alter the AI's steering and throttle output in real time.

# Aerodynamics System Spec: Spoilers, Active Airbrakes & Slipstreaming Dynamics

## 1. Core Aerodynamics Framework
Aerodynamic forces must dynamically scale with the square of vehicle speed ($F \propto v^2$), directly impacting top speed, stability, and high-speed cornering grip.

* **Base Aero Parameters per Vehicle / Part:**
  * `base_drag_coefficient` ($C_d$): Determines straight-line resistance and terminal velocity.
  * `base_downforce_coefficient` ($C_l$): Downward force applied per axle (front vs. rear balance) to increase tire normal load without adding physical mass.
* **Component-Based Modifiers:**
  * Equipping/upgrading spoilers, splitters, diffusers, and wings alters drag and downforce values proportionally.
  * Excessive rear downforce without front balance induces high-speed understeer; excessive front downforce induces high-speed oversteer.

## 2. Active Aero & Dynamic Airbrakes
Subtle, functional active aero elements that articulate based on braking threshold, speed, and lateral stability.

* **Trigger Conditions:**
  * **Threshold Braking:** Airbrakes deploy during hard braking events above a minimum speed (e.g., $> 70\text{ mph}$ / $110\text{ km/h}$).
  * **Spin / Instability Mitigation (NASCAR-style Roof/Cowl Flaps):** If yaw rate or angle of attack exceeds a threshold (car turning sideways or backwards at high speed), flaps pop out automatically to disrupt lift and keep the car planted.
* **Mechanical Effects While Deployed:**
  * Dramatic spike in drag ($+50\text{–}150\%$ temporary drag increase) to scrub straight-line speed.
  * Shift in aerodynamic balance toward the rear to stabilize braking zones and prevent spin-outs.
* **Visual Representation:**
  * Distinct pixel-art sprite animation: wings tipping up into vertical airbrake position, or roof/decklid flaps flipping open, returning flush once the event concludes.

## 3. Slipstreaming & Drafting Dynamics
A dynamic slipstream wake model applied behind moving vehicles.

* **Wake Cone Definition:**
  * A projection cone extending behind every moving vehicle, scaling in length and width with vehicle velocity and its frontal cross-section.
  * Wake Length: e.g., $10\text{m}$ at medium speeds up to $40\text{m+}$ at high speeds.
* **Slipstream Effects on Following Car:**
  * **Drag Reduction:** Trailing within the wake reduces drag ($15\text{–}35\%$ reduction based on proximity and center-line alignment), allowing higher acceleration and higher terminal velocity to initiate an overtake.
  * **Downforce Penalty (Dirty Air):** While in the turbulent wake, the following car suffers a slight downforce reduction ($10\text{–}20\%$), reducing front-end grip and requiring earlier turn-in or throttle moderation in high-speed bends.
* **Visual/Audio Cue:**
  * Subtle wind streaks/trails peeling off the leading car's trailing edges.
  * Shift in wind audio tone (muffled rushing air) when locked inside the drafting pocket.
